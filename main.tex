\title{\bfseries Per-variant tissue assignment by a sequence-to-function model is
limited by effect detectability more than by assay coverage}

\author[1]{Ronnel Davis}
\affil[1]{\small Independent researcher. Correspondence: \texttt{info@onvo.ai}}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Sequence-to-function models predict tissue-resolved regulatory activity directly from
DNA, and so can be asked, for any individual non-coding risk variant, in which tissue it
acts. We tested how reliably this works by asking whether AlphaGenome recovers
trait--tissue relationships that are already established by orthogonal evidence. Using
fine-mapped credible sets from \NTRAITS{} GWAS of \NUNIQUE{} complex traits and a shared
background of \NBG{} variants placed near GWAS loci, we scored every variant across
\NTISSUE{} tissue groups. The pre-specified expected tissue ranked first for \NHITONE{} of
\NTRAITS{} trait--GWAS pairs ($P = \PHITONE$ against a chance rate of $1/\NTISSUE$), fell
within the top three for \NHITTHREE{}, and was significantly enriched over background for
\NSIG{} (Benjamini--Hochberg $q < 0.05$). Recovery was therefore substantially better than
chance but far from universal, which let us ask what separates the traits that work from
those that do not. We found no evidence that it is how well the expected tissue is
measured: across traits, the number of tracks available for the expected tissue was
uncorrelated with that tissue's enrichment ($\rho = \RHOTRACK$, $P = \PTRACK$) or rank
($\rho = \RHOTRACKRANK$, $P = \PTRACKRANK$), although with \NTRAITS{} trait--GWAS pairs
and eight distinct expected tissues this test can exclude only a strong coverage effect.
Brain has \BRAINHIST{} histone and \BRAINACC{} accessibility tracks, yet both
brain-expected traits fail; the pancreatic islet has \ISLETHIST{} and \ISLETACC{}, yet
type 2 diabetes ranks the islet first in one GWAS (non-significantly, $z = \TDZ$,
$P = \TDP$) and \TDXRANK{}th in another. What did track significant enrichment was a
tissue-agnostic quantity, effect visibility: whether the model predicted any regulatory
effect for a trait's variants at all ($\rho = \RHOVIS$ with expected-tissue enrichment,
$P = \PVIS$). Much of that association is expected by construction, because predicted
histone effects are strongly shared across tissues (mean inter-tissue correlation
\INTERTISSUE{}), and visibility did not significantly predict the expected tissue's rank
across traits ($\rho = \RHOVISRANK$, $P = \PVISRANK$). At the level of individual
variants, however, the expected tissue ranked first \QHIGHRATIO{} times more often than a
calibrated null among the most visible quartile of variants, against
\QLOWRATIO{}--\QTHREERATIO{} times elsewhere (within-trait odds ratio \ORTOPQ{},
$P = \PTOPQ$). Run matched against LDSC-SEG \citep{finucane2018seg}---same \NMATCH{}
traits, same GWAS datasets, same \NTISSUE{} tissue definitions---the two methods tied on
rank-1 recovery (\AGONE{} and \LDONE{} of \NMATCH{}), with LDSC-SEG ahead on top-three
recovery (\LDTHREE{} versus \AGTHREE{}) and on rank-1 recoveries that were also
significant (\LDONESIG{} versus \AGONESIG{}). Their failures are complementary: LDSC-SEG
recovers the islet for type 2 diabetes where AlphaGenome ranks it \TWODAGRANK{}th, while
AlphaGenome recovers artery for coronary artery disease where LDSC-SEG finds no
significant tissue. On \NSAMEWRONG{} of the \NBOTHMISS{} traits defeating both, the
methods independently pick the same wrong tissue, indicating our expected-tissue labels
limit the benchmark. We conclude that a null tissue assignment from these models more
often reflects undetected variant effects than absent tissue involvement, that the
tissue-specific component of a predicted variant effect is small relative to the shared
one, and that expanding assay coverage for under-measured tissues is unlikely, on its
own, to fix it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Most risk variants for common disease are non-coding \citep{maurano2012regulatory, finucane2015partitioning}.
Interpreting them requires knowing not only the target gene but the cell type in which
the variant acts, since a regulatory element is functional only in tissues that use it.
The established approach is statistical: partition trait heritability across
tissue-specific annotations and test for enrichment
\citep{finucane2015partitioning, finucane2018seg}. These methods consume whole GWAS
summary statistics and require matched functional annotations for each tissue.

Sequence-to-function models offer a different route. Enformer
\citep{avsec2021enformer}, Borzoi \citep{linder2025borzoi} and AlphaGenome
\citep{avsec2026alphagenome} predict functional genomic tracks directly from sequence.
AlphaGenome accepts one megabase of DNA and predicts thousands of tracks spanning
expression, accessibility, histone modification, transcription factor binding, contact
maps and splicing at up to base-pair resolution. Because one model emits predictions for
many tissues at once, contrasting reference and alternate alleles yields a per-tissue
effect estimate for a \emph{single} variant---something heritability partitioning
cannot provide, as it operates on genome-wide aggregates.

Whether those per-variant tissue assignments are trustworthy at the level of a whole
trait is largely untested. This matters practically: a null prediction is easily
misread as evidence that a tissue is \emph{not} involved, when it may instead mean the
model failed to detect the variant's effect in any tissue. Distinguishing these
requires a benchmark with known answers, and---if recovery turns out to be partial---an
account of what separates success from failure.

We built such a benchmark and used it to adjudicate between two candidate explanations
for failure. The \emph{assay-coverage} hypothesis holds that a tissue is recovered when
the model has seen enough experimental data for it. The \emph{detectability} hypothesis
holds that recovery depends on whether the trait's variants produce predicted effects
large enough to rank tissues at all, independently of which tissue is expected. These
make opposite predictions for well-instrumented tissues paired with subtle traits. We find
no support for the first and partial support for the second, with an important
qualification: most of what the model predicts for a variant is shared across tissues,
and the tissue-specific remainder is small.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Benchmark design}

We assembled \NTRAITS{} trait--GWAS pairs covering \NUNIQUE{} traits (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:traits}) whose principal tissue
is supported by prior literature, spanning liver, immune tissue, heart, artery, brain,
lung and skin, plus type 2 diabetes (T2D). For each trait we took fine-mapped 95\%
credible sets from the Open Targets Platform \citep{buniello2025opentargets} and kept the
highest-posterior non-coding variant per independent signal, capped at \NCAP{} signals
per trait. The Platform's fine-mapping method depends on what it has ingested for a
study: SuSiE-inf \citep{wang2020susie} where full summary statistics are available,
otherwise PICS applied either to summary statistics or, when none were ingested, to the
reported lead variants alone. In our panel \NSUSIE{} studies are SuSiE-based,
\NPICSSUM{} are PICS on summary statistics and \NPICSTOP{} are PICS around reported top
hits (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:traits}). For the last group the highest-posterior variant
is in practice the reported lead SNP, so for those traits the benchmark scores GWAS lead
variants rather than fine-mapped causal candidates.

Two points about provenance, because the benchmark is easy to misread. First, the Open
Targets Platform supplies \emph{only the variant list}. It makes no tissue predictions,
and its locus-to-gene scores are not used anywhere in the primary analysis, which is
histone-based and involves no gene assignment. Every tissue ranking reported here is
AlphaGenome's alone. Second, the expected tissue for each trait is a label we assigned
from prior literature before any scoring, not a quantity derived from data in this study.
The benchmark therefore measures agreement between AlphaGenome and an external
literature consensus, and the labels are the weakest link in it: they are judgements, and
Section~\ref{sec:ldsc} shows two of them are contradicted by both methods.

Each variant was scored with AlphaGenome over a \SI{1}{\mega\bp} window. Histone
modification tracks are the primary readout: the RNA-seq scorer requires choosing a
target gene per variant, and any such rule differs systematically between trait
variants (which have a nominated gene) and background variants (which do not), biasing
the comparison. Histone centre-mask scores involve no such choice. We used the five
marks present in all tissue groups (H3K27ac, H3K36me3, H3K4me1, H3K4me3, H3K9ac).

Absolute calibration requires a null, so we generated a single shared background of
\NBG{} single-nucleotide variants placed \SIrange{50}{400}{\kilo\bp} from T2D and
LDL-cholesterol signals on the same chromosomes, with reference alleles from GRCh38. The
background is not matched to trait variants on allele frequency, linkage disequilibrium
or annotation, so enrichment over it partly reflects that GWAS variants sit in regulatory
sequence at all; the rank of the expected tissue among the eleven is the measure that
speaks to tissue identity. Every per-tissue score is standardised against this
background, so each trait is scored independently.

\subsection{Recovery is well above chance but incomplete}

The expected tissue ranked first for \NHITONE{} of \NTRAITS{} trait--GWAS pairs against
\CHANCEONE{} expected by chance ($P = \PHITONE$), and within the top three for
\NHITTHREE{} against \CHANCETHREE{} ($P = \PHITTHREE$). Type 2 diabetes contributes two
pairs; counting it once leaves the conclusion unchanged ($P = \PHITONEUNIQ$ or
$\PHITONEUNIQB$ depending on which dataset is kept). A null that resamples background
variant sets of the same size, and so allows for unequal tail behaviour across tissues,
gives an expected \CHANCECAL{} rank-1 recoveries rather than \CHANCEONE{}. Judged by
enrichment rather than rank, the expected tissue was significantly above background for
\NSIG{} pairs (BH $q < 0.05$). Table~\ref{tab:results} gives the full ranking with, for
each pair, the proportion of variant resamples in which the expected tissue stays first;
\NFRAGILE{} of the \NHITONE{} rank-1 recoveries have bootstrap support below one half.
Figure~\ref{fig:heat} shows the trait-by-tissue matrix.

Liver traits recovered liver (alanine aminotransferase $z = \ALTZ$), cardiac traits
recovered heart, and platelet count and the Crohn's disease medication proxy recovered
immune tissue.
Rank and enrichment do not always agree: ulcerative colitis ranks the expected tissue
first while showing no enrichment at all ($z \approx 0$), because when every tissue sits
near zero the ordering is noise. We therefore report both, and we do not count ulcerative
colitis as a success anywhere below: its GWAS has only \UCCASES{} cases, and its credible
sets are almost all single variants at marginal significance (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:traits}), so the input variant set itself is suspect.

\subsection{Assay depth does not explain which traits are recovered}

If recovery were limited by how well a tissue is measured, the expected tissue's
enrichment should rise with the number of tracks available for it. We find no evidence
that it does. Across trait--GWAS pairs, enrichment was uncorrelated with the expected
tissue's histone track count ($\rho = \RHOTRACK$, $P = \PTRACK$) and with its chromatin
accessibility track count ($\rho = \RHOACC$, $P = \PACC$); both point estimates are, if
anything, negative. The same holds for rank ($\rho = \RHOTRACKRANK$, $P = \PTRACKRANK$;
accessibility $\rho = \RHOACCRANK$, $P = \PACCRANK$). This is a low-powered test: track
count takes only eight distinct values across the \NTRAITS{} pairs, because traits that
share an expected tissue share a count, and the 95\% confidence interval on the first
correlation runs from \RHOTRACKLO{} to \RHOTRACKHI{}. The data exclude a strong coverage
dependence, not a moderate one.

The extremes make the point directly. Brain is among the best-instrumented groups
(\BRAINHIST{} histone tracks, \BRAINFIVE{} of them among the five marks scored here, and
\BRAINACC{} accessibility tracks), yet both brain-expected traits fail: body mass index
ranks the brain \BMIRANK{}rd ($z = \BMIZ$) and the cognition/education/schizophrenia
meta-analysis shows no brain enrichment at all ($z = \COGZ$). The pancreatic islet is the
sparsest group in the panel (\ISLETHIST{} histone tracks, \ISLETACC{} accessibility
track, no ATAC), yet T2D ranks the islet first in the larger GWAS. That recovery is weak
(non-significant, bootstrap support \TDBOOT{}) and is not reproduced in the second T2D
dataset (Section~\ref{sec:t2d}), so the islet example shows only that sparse coverage does
not preclude recovery, not that coverage is irrelevant.

\subsection{Effect detectability explains significance; its link to tissue identity is weaker}

We defined a tissue-agnostic measure of \emph{effect visibility}: the mean predicted
effect of a trait's variants across all tissue-assigned histone tracks, standardised
against the same background. This quantity does not reference the expected tissue.

Visibility tracks expected-tissue enrichment closely ($\rho = \RHOVIS$, $P = \PVIS$).
Traits whose expected tissue was significantly enriched had median visibility \VISREC{},
against \VISNOT{} for the rest ($P = \PVISMWU$). Much of this association is, however,
expected by construction. Predicted histone effects are strongly shared across tissues:
over the \NVAR{} scored variants the mean pairwise inter-tissue correlation of
standardised effects is \INTERTISSUE{} (range \INTERTISSUEMIN{}--\INTERTISSUEMAX{}), and
a single component explains \PCONE{}\% of their variance. Visibility therefore correlates
with the enrichment of \emph{whichever} tissue ranks first ($\rho = \RHOVISTOP$,
$P = \PVISTOP$) as strongly as with the expected tissue's. A trait whose variants have
large predicted effects will show significant enrichment of its expected tissue almost
regardless of whether the model has located the tissue correctly.

The scale-free question is whether visibility predicts the expected tissue's
\emph{rank}. Across trait--GWAS pairs it does not, significantly: $\rho = \RHOVISRANK$
($P = \PVISRANK$) with rank, $\rho = \RHOVISBOOT$ ($P = \PVISBOOT$) with bootstrap
support for rank~1, and median visibility \VISRANKONE{} for rank-1 pairs against
\VISRANKNOT{} for the rest ($P = \PVISRANKMWU$). Of the ten most visible pairs, \NTOPVISHIT{} rank the expected tissue first, as do
\NBOTVISHIT{} of the ten least visible.

At the level of individual variants the picture is sharper. For each scored variant we
asked whether the expected tissue ranked first among the eleven, against a null
calibrated on background variants (which is not uniform: immune tissue ranks first for
\IMMUNENULL{}\% of background variants, liver for \LIVERNULL{}\%). Overall the expected
tissue ranked first for \ALLOBS{}\% of variants against \ALLNULL{}\% expected, a
\ALLRATIO{}-fold excess. The excess is concentrated in the most visible variants:
\QHIGHRATIO{}-fold in the top visibility quartile ($P = \QHIGHP$) against \QLOWRATIO{},
\QTWORATIO{} and \QTHREERATIO{}-fold in the lower three. Within traits, variants in a
trait's own top visibility quartile were \ORTOPQ{} times as likely to rank the expected
tissue first as the trait's other variants (logistic regression with trait fixed effects,
95\% CI \ORTOPQLO{}--\ORTOPQHI{}, $P = \PTOPQ$; \ORVISSD{} per standard deviation of
visibility, $P = \PVISSD$). Tissue information in the predictions is therefore real but
modest, and it is carried mainly by the variants with the largest predicted effects.
Where a trait has few such variants, its tissue ranking rests on a handful of loci, which
is why several rank-1 recoveries have low bootstrap support.

\subsection{Head-to-head against LDSC-SEG}\label{sec:ldsc}

The established method for this question is stratified LD score regression applied to
specifically expressed genes (LDSC-SEG) \citep{finucane2018seg}. Unlike AlphaGenome it
never scores an individual variant: it asks whether a trait's heritability, aggregated
across all SNPs, is concentrated near the genes a tissue expresses distinctively. If the
detectability account is right, that difference should matter precisely where per-variant
effects are too small to detect.

We ran it matched: the \NMATCH{} traits for which harmonised summary statistics were
available from the GWAS Catalog, the same GWAS datasets as the AlphaGenome benchmark, and
the same \NTISSUE{} tissue definitions. We built the LDSC-SEG annotations from GTEx v8 median
expression ourselves so that both methods partition the genome into identical tissue
groups, rather than comparing against the 205-annotation taxonomy of the published
analysis. Table~\ref{tab:ldsc} gives the result.

\textbf{Neither method dominates.} The expected tissue ranked first for \AGONE{} of
\NMATCH{} traits under AlphaGenome and \LDONE{} of \NMATCH{} under LDSC-SEG, both far
above the \CHANCEMATCH{} expected by chance ($P = \PMATCH$ each). LDSC-SEG holds the edge
on top-three recovery (\LDTHREE{} versus \AGTHREE{}) and on rank-1 recoveries that are
also significant for the expected tissue (\LDONESIG{} versus \AGONESIG{}; AlphaGenome's
rank-1 calls for ulcerative colitis and atrial fibrillation are not significant). We
expected the established method to dominate; it is ahead, but not by much.

What the methods have instead is \emph{complementary} failure, and the two sharpest cases
point in opposite directions. For type 2 diabetes LDSC-SEG ranks the islet first
($P = \TWODLDP$) where AlphaGenome ranks it \TWODAGRANK{}th---the aggregate method recovers
islet biology the literature independently supports \citep{gaulton2010islet,
pasquali2014enhancer, chiou2021islet} from variants whose individual effects AlphaGenome
cannot resolve. That is the detectability account's clearest confirmation. For coronary
artery disease the ordering reverses: AlphaGenome ranks artery first with significant
enrichment ($q = \CADAGQ$) while LDSC-SEG ranks it \CADLDRANK{}rd behind lung and fat,
with no tissue reaching significance (best $P = \CADLDTOPP$). Whatever separates success
from failure, it is not that one method is uniformly better resolved. (Ulcerative colitis
also splits the methods on rank, immune first under AlphaGenome and \UCLDRANK{}th under
LDSC-SEG, but neither method finds any enrichment for it and we treat it as a joint
failure of the input GWAS.)

The two also fail \emph{together} informatively. Both methods miss on \NBOTHMISS{} traits, and on
\NSAMEWRONG{} of those the methods independently choose the same wrong tissue: both rank
fat above liver for HDL cholesterol, and both put immune tissue first for atopic
dermatitis, AlphaGenome with its strongest enrichment in the panel ($z = \ADAGZ$) and
LDSC-SEG with $P = \ADLDP$. When two methods sharing almost no machinery agree against
our label, the label is the likelier error. This is the strongest evidence here
that the pre-specified expected tissues, not the methods, limit the benchmark.

\subsection{Type 2 diabetes is unstable, not null}\label{sec:t2d}

T2D behaves as the detectability account predicts, and its behaviour is a caution
against over-reading single-dataset results. In the larger GWAS
\citep{mahajan2018finemap} the islet ranks first, consistent with the substantial
literature localising T2D risk to islet regulatory elements
\citep{gaulton2010islet, pasquali2014enhancer, greenwald2019islet, chiou2021islet}---but
the enrichment is modest and does not reach significance ($z = \TDZ$, $P = \TDP$). In an
independent GWAS \citep{xue2018t2d} the islet ranks \TDXRANK{}th ($z = \TDXZ$).

The two variant sets share only \OVERLAP{} variants. They differ in GWAS and in
fine-mapping method (PICS around reported lead variants for the larger dataset, SuSiE-inf
on summary statistics for the other), so they are near-disjoint samples of the same
underlying signal, and the disagreement is partly a statement about how much the scored
variant depends on those choices. The matched LDSC-SEG run (Section~\ref{sec:ldsc})
resolves what AlphaGenome alone could not: on the same Xue dataset where AlphaGenome ranks
the islet \TWODAGRANK{}th, LDSC-SEG ranks it \emph{first} ($P = \TWODLDP$, nominal, with
bulk pancreas standing in for islet). The islet signal is present in that GWAS; AlphaGenome
does not detect it in the fine-mapped variants. That upgrades our reading of T2D from
``uninformative'' to a specific negative---per-variant prediction misses islet enrichment
that aggregate heritability partitioning recovers from the same study---and is consistent
with the instability across the two T2D datasets reflecting the model's detection
threshold rather than a genuine absence of islet involvement.

One caveat compounds this. The larger dataset used islet epigenome maps in its own fine
mapping \citep{mahajan2018finemap}. Open Targets has not ingested its summary statistics,
so the credible sets we used are PICS sets built around the reported index variants. The
annotation-informed credible sets of the original study therefore do not enter, but the
index variants come from a study whose signal definition was islet-aware, and any residual
influence would bias \emph{towards} islet variants---so the islet-first result in that
dataset should be treated as an upper bound, not a confirmation.

\section{Discussion}

Our benchmark supports a narrow claim with practical consequences. AlphaGenome's
per-variant tissue assignments recover independently established trait--tissue
relationships far above chance, and can be used to generate tissue hypotheses for
individual non-coding variants. But significant recovery is conditional on the model predicting a detectable effect for
those variants, and \NNOTSIG{} of our \NTRAITS{} trait--GWAS pairs fall below that
threshold. For those the tissue ranking rests on a handful of loci and carries little
information, and crucially, nothing in the output distinguishes this state from a
confident negative. A second point follows from the structure of the predictions
themselves: the tissue-specific part of a predicted variant effect is small next to the
part shared across tissues, so a per-variant tissue call is a weak signal even when the
variant is visible, and it should be aggregated over loci before it is trusted.

That the limiting factor looks like detectability rather than assay coverage is the more
useful finding, because it redirects effort. Adding chromatin accessibility assays for
under-measured tissues would be a natural response to a coverage-limited failure; our
results suggest it would not, by itself, rescue the traits that fail here, since the
best-instrumented tissue in the panel hosts two of the clearest failures. The relevant
axis is instead the size of the regulatory effects a trait's variants actually have.
Highly polygenic traits---body mass index, cognition, T2D---are those whose individual
variants have small effects, and they are among the ones that fail, although the pattern
is not clean: platelet count is at least as polygenic and is recovered strongly. Two
alternative readings of low visibility cannot be excluded by this design. The scored
variant may not be the causal one, since \NPICSTOP{} of our \NTRAITS{} variant sets are
lead SNPs rather than fine-mapped candidates, and visibility rises weakly with posterior
inclusion probability at the variant level ($\rho = \RHOVPIP$, $P = \PVPIP$); and the
signal itself may be marginal, since visibility also rises with GWAS significance
($\rho = \RHOVNEGP$ per variant, $P = \PVNEGP$) and the two least visible traits come
from GWAS with fewer than four thousand cases. Beyond these, the brain failures may
reflect that the relevant cell types and developmental stages are poorly represented by
bulk adult brain tracks. The detectability reading is consistent with independent reports
that current sequence models capture promoter-proximal determinants well but distal
enhancer effects poorly \citep{karollus2023promoters}, and explain inter-individual
expression variation weakly \citep{sasse2023benchmark, huang2023personal}.

For T2D specifically, the biological picture in the literature is not a single tissue
in any case: T2D loci partition into mechanistically distinct clusters, only a subset of
which are beta-cell mediated \citep{udler2018clusters, lotta2017ir}. A single expected
tissue label is a simplification for this trait in a way it is not for alanine
aminotransferase, and this may contribute to its low visibility.

\subsection*{What would sharpen this}

Three follow-ups would strengthen or overturn these conclusions. First, extend the
matched comparison to brain-expected traits. The specific body mass index and cognition
GWAS used here are not distributed with summary statistics through the GWAS Catalog, but
other large GWAS of both traits are, and rerunning both methods on one of those is the
obvious next step; this is the one place the detectability account remains untested
against the reference method. Second,
aggregate over full credible sets weighted by posterior probability instead of taking
the top variant, which would quantify how much of the failure is fine-mapping
instability---the near-disjoint T2D variant sets suggest this is substantial. Third,
test the detectability account prospectively: rank traits by visibility before looking
at tissue assignments, and check that the predicted recovery ordering holds on a held-out
trait set. Supplying external islet accessibility data \citep{chiou2021islet} remains
worth doing, but our results reframe it as a test of the coverage hypothesis rather than
an expected fix.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{One variant per signal.} We scored the highest-posterior variant per
credible set; the median 95\% credible set in the SuSiE-fine-mapped T2D replication data
contains \CSMEDIAN{} variants, so the causal variant is often a different member.
\item \textbf{Bulk tissue dilutes cell types.} Most tracks are bulk tissue, and
attenuation of cell-type-specific effects is not equal across groups.
\item \textbf{Expected tissues are author-assigned judgements.} This is the benchmark's
principal weakness. Labels were fixed before scoring and drawn from prior literature, but
they are not derived from data and several traits have more than one defensible principal
tissue (atopic dermatitis: skin and immune; coronary artery disease: artery and liver;
alkaline phosphatase: liver and bone). The LDSC-SEG comparison contradicts two of the twenty
(Section~\ref{sec:ldsc}); others may be equally contestable. We report rank rather than a
binary hit partly for this reason. A benchmark with labels derived from an independent
quantitative method, rather than assigned by hand, would be stronger.
\item \textbf{Proxy phenotypes and underpowered GWAS.} Several traits are PheCode or
medication-proxy definitions rather than curated case--control phenotypes. The type 1
diabetes entry is the PheCode for type 1 diabetes \emph{with ophthalmic manifestations}
(\TODCASES{} cases), the ulcerative colitis entry has \UCCASES{} cases, and the Crohn's
disease entry is a FinnGen first-line-medication endpoint with \CROHNCASES{} cases, which
is far broader than Crohn's disease. The cognition entry is a pleiotropic meta-analysis of
cognitive ability, educational attainment and schizophrenia. Case counts and definitions
are given in Table~\ref{tab:traits}. The two smallest GWAS yield credible sets that are
almost all singletons at marginal significance, and their failures should be read as
failures of the input rather than of the model.
\item \textbf{Rank is unstable when enrichment is flat.} Where all tissues sit near
zero, the top-ranked tissue is arbitrary; this is why we report enrichment and
significance alongside rank.
\item \textbf{Skewed effect distributions.} Median per-variant scores are near or below
zero for every trait; enrichment is driven by a minority of loci. Means are appropriate
for enrichment but describe the tail.
\item \textbf{The matched comparison contains no brain traits.} Summary statistics for
the body mass index and cognition GWAS used here are not distributed through the GWAS
Catalog, so the two traits that fail most clearly under AlphaGenome were not tested
against LDSC-SEG; other GWAS of both traits with public summary statistics exist and
should be used. Type 2 diabetes
is the substitute case, but the detectability account's strongest evidence remains
untested head-to-head.
\item \textbf{The LDSC-SEG islet annotation is bulk pancreas.} GTEx has no islet tissue,
so on the LDSC-SEG side the islet group is whole pancreas---the same cell-type dilution
affecting the AlphaGenome side, and a conservative bias for the diabetes result.
\item \textbf{Some LDSC-SEG ranks rest on non-significant coefficients.} Where no tissue
reaches significance the ordering is arbitrary, exactly as for AlphaGenome; ulcerative
colitis and coronary artery disease are the clearest cases.
\item \textbf{LDSC required patching to run.} The Python~3 port used here contained
defects that silently altered results: chromosome-split LD scores were read for a single
chromosome instead of 22, and cell-type $P$-values were two-tailed where the published
method is one-tailed. Both were corrected and validated (Methods).
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous fine-mapping.} Credible sets come from three regimes
(SuSiE-inf, PICS on summary statistics, PICS around reported lead variants), and for
\NPICSTOP{} of \NTRAITS{} datasets the scored variant is effectively the GWAS lead SNP.
Trait-to-trait differences in visibility therefore partly reflect how well the scored
variant stands in for the causal one.
\item \textbf{Unmatched background.} The background is a single set of \NBG{} SNVs near
T2D and LDL loci, not matched on allele frequency, LD or annotation. Enrichment over it
mixes tissue identity with the generic regulatory character of GWAS variants; rank and
the calibrated per-variant analysis are the tissue-specific readouts.
\item \textbf{Low power for the coverage test.} With \NTRAITS{} pairs and eight distinct
expected tissues, the correlation between track count and recovery can exclude only a
strong coverage effect.
\item \textbf{Single model, single readout.} One model, histone tracks only. Whether
Enformer or Borzoi show the same detectability dependence is untested.
\item \textbf{Predictions, not measurements.} Every quantity is a model output; none is
experimentally validated here.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Variant selection.} Fine-mapped credible sets were retrieved from the Open
Targets Platform GraphQL API \citep{buniello2025opentargets}. For each trait we
identified GWAS studies mapped to the trait's ontology term, selected the study with the
most credible sets, and retrieved up to 400. Within each set we kept members flagged as
belonging to the 95\% credible set and took the variant with highest posterior inclusion
probability. Signals whose highest-posterior variant had a coding most-severe consequence (missense,
stop, frameshift, synonymous, canonical splice, in-frame) were dropped rather than
replaced by the next non-coding member. Remaining variants were ordered by signal
significance and capped at \NCAP{} per trait. Fine-mapping method and confidence for each
study were retrieved from the same API and are listed in Table~\ref{tab:traits}. The
cognition study was retrieved under the schizophrenia ontology term; it is the Lam et al.\
pleiotropic meta-analysis of cognitive ability, educational attainment and schizophrenia.

\paragraph{Background construction.} For each non-coding T2D and LDL-cholesterol signal
we drew two positions offset by \SIrange{50}{400}{\kilo\bp} in either direction on the
same chromosome, and kept 320 at random. Reference bases were retrieved in batch from the
Ensembl GRCh38 REST API and each position converted to a transition SNV; \NBG{} passed
retrieval and scoring. The same background serves every trait.

\paragraph{Scoring.} AlphaGenome (\texttt{alphagenome} Python client) was queried with
\texttt{score\_variant} over \texttt{SEQUENCE\_LENGTH\_1MB} intervals centred on each
variant, using the recommended \texttt{CHIP\_HISTONE} centre-mask and \texttt{RNA\_SEQ}
gene-mask scorers. Histone scores form the primary analysis.

\paragraph{Tissue grouping.} Output metadata was grouped into \NTISSUE{} tissue classes
by biosample ontology name and GTEx tissue label \citep{gtex2020, encode2020}, restricted
to histone marks present in all groups.

\paragraph{Statistics.} For tissue $g$ and variant $v$ the raw score is the mean absolute
predicted effect over the tracks of $g$, standardised as
$z = (x - \mu_g^{\mathrm{bg}})/\sigma_g^{\mathrm{bg}}$. Trait tissue scores are means of
$z$ over variants. Enrichment used one-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ against background with
Benjamini--Hochberg correction across the \NTRAITS{} traits. Effect visibility is the
same statistic computed over all tissue-assigned histone tracks pooled, standardised
against the pooled background. Associations used Spearman correlation. Recovery rates
were tested against $1/\NTISSUE$ and $3/\NTISSUE$ by exact binomial test, treating
trait--GWAS pairs as independent; a calibrated chance rate was obtained by resampling
background variant sets of size 150 (4{,}000 draws) and recording which tissue ranked
first. Bootstrap support for a rank-1 recovery is the proportion of 2{,}000 resamples of a
trait's variants (with replacement) in which the expected tissue ranks first. Per-variant
analyses use the same standardised scores: the rank of the expected tissue among the
\NTISSUE{} groups for each variant, a null rate equal to the frequency with which that
tissue ranks first among background variants, quartiles of visibility pooled across
traits, and logistic regression of rank-1 status on visibility with trait fixed effects.
Inter-tissue correlation is the mean pairwise Spearman correlation of standardised
per-variant scores across the \NTISSUE{} groups, and the shared component is the first
principal component of that matrix. These analyses are implemented in
\texttt{scripts/37\_review\_analyses.py}.

\paragraph{Data availability.} All inputs are public: GWAS summary statistics via the
accessions in Table~\ref{tab:traits}, fine-mapped credible sets via the Open Targets
Platform API, and AlphaGenome predictions via the model's public API. Analysis scripts
and derived score matrices are available from the author on request.
